Freelancer vs Agency vs DIY Website Builder: Real Cost Breakdown

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 6 min read
DIY builders are cheapest ($16-$60/month) but cost you the most time; freelancers offer the best value for most small businesses ($500-$3,000 one-time); agencies cost more ($3,000-$15,000+) but suit businesses needing ongoing strategy and larger scope.

DIY website builders

Cost: $16-$60/month (Wix, Squarespace, or similar). What you're really paying with: your own time. Building a genuinely polished, converting website yourself — writing copy, choosing images, structuring pages for SEO — typically takes far longer than people expect, especially if you're doing it around an already-busy schedule.

Best for: businesses with a very simple site need, comfort with basic design tools, and time to invest upfront.

Freelancers

Cost: typically $500-$3,000 for a small business site, depending on scope and the freelancer's experience level. What you're paying for: direct, personal attention from one person who handles your whole project, usually at a lower rate than an agency because there's no team overhead.

Best for: most small businesses — you get professional quality without agency-level pricing, as long as you vet the freelancer's past work carefully.

Agencies

Cost: generally $3,000-$15,000+ depending on scope. What you're paying for: a full team (design, development, sometimes copywriting and strategy), more structured project management, and often more capacity for larger or more complex builds.

Best for: businesses with bigger budgets, more complex requirements, or ongoing marketing needs beyond just the website itself.

The real comparison isn't just price

It's price relative to what you actually need. A simple one-page site doesn't need agency-level resources. A complex multi-feature e-commerce platform with ongoing marketing needs often does need more than a single freelancer can realistically provide alone.

How to vet whichever route you choose

Ask for a portfolio of real, live sites (not mockups), check if those sites still look current and functional, and ask directly about post-launch support — that's where quality differences show up most clearly.

Is a freelancer riskier than an agency?

Not inherently — it depends on the individual. Check reviews, past work, and communication responsiveness before committing either way.

Can I start with DIY and upgrade to a freelancer or agency later?

Yes, this is common and reasonable — many businesses validate their idea with a simple DIY site, then invest in a professional rebuild once they have traction.
