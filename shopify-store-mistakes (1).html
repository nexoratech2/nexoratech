7 Mistakes Small Businesses Make With Their Shopify Store

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 6 min read
The most common Shopify mistakes are skipping proper product photography, ignoring mobile checkout experience, no abandoned cart follow-up, weak product descriptions, poor site speed from too many apps, missing trust signals, and no clear return policy.

1. Skipping proper product photography

Blurry, inconsistent, or low-resolution product photos are one of the fastest ways to lose a sale — customers can't touch the product, so photos are doing all the trust-building work images normally can't replace with words alone.

2. Ignoring the mobile checkout experience

The majority of e-commerce traffic is mobile, yet many stores are tested and polished mainly on desktop. A clunky mobile checkout — small buttons, forms that don't autofill correctly — quietly kills conversions that never get flagged as a problem.

3. No abandoned cart follow-up

A large share of shoppers add items to cart and leave without buying. Shopify makes abandoned cart email recovery relatively simple to set up, yet many stores never turn it on — leaving recoverable revenue on the table.

4. Weak or generic product descriptions

Descriptions that just restate specs ("100% cotton, machine washable") miss the chance to answer the real question customers have: why should I buy this one? Good descriptions address the actual problem the product solves.

5. Too many apps slowing down the site

Every app adds some load time. Stacking ten apps for features you barely use creates a slow, laggy store — and slow sites measurably lose sales, especially on mobile connections.

6. Missing trust signals

No visible reviews, no clear "About" story, no security badges near checkout — small trust gaps that add up, especially for a store customers haven't heard of before.

7. No clear shipping or return policy

Uncertainty about returns is one of the biggest hesitations for online shoppers. A clear, easy-to-find policy removes a major purchase objection before it even comes up.

Which of these mistakes matters most to fix first?

Mobile checkout and product photography tend to have the most direct impact on conversion rate — start there before tackling the smaller trust-signal fixes.

Can these fixes be done without a developer?

Most of them, yes — photography, descriptions, and enabling abandoned cart emails are manageable directly in Shopify's admin. App audits and speed fixes sometimes benefit from a professional review.
