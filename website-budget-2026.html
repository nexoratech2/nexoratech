How Much Should You Budget for a Professional Website in 2026?

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 5 min read
Most small businesses should budget $800-$3,000 for a professional website in 2026; simple landing pages can run under $500, while complex e-commerce or custom-functionality sites often run $3,000-$10,000+.

Why "how much does a website cost" has no single answer

A website is really a range of possible products — a one-page landing site and a full e-commerce platform with custom features are both "websites," but they're entirely different projects in scope. The honest answer is always "it depends on what you need it to do," so here's how to actually estimate that.

By project type

  • Simple landing page (1-3 pages): $300-$800
  • Standard business website (5-10 pages, contact forms, basic SEO): $800-$2,500
  • E-commerce store (product catalog, checkout, inventory): $1,500-$5,000
  • Custom functionality (booking systems, membership areas, complex integrations): $5,000-$15,000+

What actually drives the price up or down

Number of unique page designs (not just page count), whether content and photography are provided or need to be created, how much custom functionality versus off-the-shelf features are used, and how much revision/iteration is included in the process.

The hidden ongoing costs

Beyond the initial build: domain registration (~$10-15/year), hosting ($5-30/month for most small business sites), and either your own time or a retainer for updates and maintenance. Budget for these separately — they're easy to forget when comparing quotes.

A practical rule of thumb

If a quote sounds dramatically cheaper than everything else you're seeing for the same scope, ask specifically what's included — the gap is usually in revisions, SEO setup, or post-launch support, not just profit margin.

Is it worth paying more for a custom-built site vs. a template?

If your brand and positioning matter to your sales (most businesses), yes — a well-customized site builds more trust and converts better than an obviously generic template.

Should I expect ongoing costs after the site is built?

Yes — at minimum hosting and domain renewal. Many businesses also budget for periodic content updates or a maintenance retainer.
