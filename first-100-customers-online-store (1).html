How to Get Your First 100 Customers for a New Online Store

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 6 min read
Getting your first 100 customers usually comes from a combination of your existing personal network, organic social content showing the product in use, small-scale targeted ads, and reaching out directly to micro-influencers or communities relevant to your niche.

Start with people who already know you

Your existing personal and professional network is the fastest, lowest-cost source of first customers — friends, past colleagues, local community groups. A direct, genuine message asking for support (and honest feedback) often converts better than any cold marketing at this early stage.

Show the product being used, not just described

Organic social content — short videos of the product in real use, behind-the-scenes of making or packing it — tends to earn more early trust than polished ad-style content, because it feels authentic rather than like a sales pitch.

Reach out directly to micro-influencers in your niche

You don't need a celebrity partnership. Small creators with a genuinely engaged (even if modest) following in your specific niche often respond to direct outreach and can meaningfully move the needle for a new store, sometimes for product exchange rather than a large fee.

Run small, targeted paid tests

Once you have some initial traction and know roughly who's buying, a modest, tightly-targeted ad budget (even $10-20/day) can validate which messaging and audience actually convert before scaling spend further.

Join and genuinely participate in relevant communities

Online communities and forums specific to your niche can be valuable — but only if you participate genuinely rather than just dropping promotional links, which usually backfires and can get you removed.

Make it easy for early customers to refer others

A simple referral incentive (a discount for both the referrer and the new customer) turns your first genuinely happy customers into a small but real acquisition channel, often the most cost-effective one available at this stage.

What to prioritize in the first 30 days

Focus on getting real customer feedback and testimonials as much as on the sales themselves — those early reviews become the trust-building asset that makes every subsequent customer acquisition effort work better.

Do I need a big budget to get my first 100 customers?

No — many of the most effective early tactics (personal network, organic content, community participation) cost time rather than money.

How long does it typically take to reach 100 customers?

It varies widely by product and effort, but consistent daily effort across these channels commonly gets a new store there within one to three months.
