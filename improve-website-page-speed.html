How to Improve Your Website's Page Speed (Beginner's Guide)

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 5 min read
The fastest wins for page speed are compressing and resizing images before uploading, removing unused apps or plugins, enabling lazy loading for images below the fold, and choosing a reliable hosting provider — most speed problems come from oversized images, not code.

Why page speed actually matters

Beyond just user experience, page speed is a direct ranking factor for Google and a direct conversion factor for visitors — slower pages measurably lose both search ranking position and sales, often without the business ever realizing it's happening.

1. Compress and resize your images

This is the single biggest speed win for most sites. A photo straight from a modern phone camera or DSLR is often 5-10x larger than what a webpage actually needs. Resizing to the actual display size and compressing before uploading can cut page weight dramatically with no visible quality loss.

2. Enable lazy loading

Lazy loading means images only load as a visitor scrolls near them, rather than all loading upfront the moment the page opens. This is especially valuable for image-heavy pages like portfolios or product galleries.

3. Remove apps and plugins you don't actually use

Every installed app or plugin adds some loading overhead, even if you're not actively using its features. Periodically auditing and removing unused ones is an easy, often-overlooked speed win.

4. Choose reliable hosting

Extremely cheap hosting plans often mean shared, overloaded servers that respond slowly regardless of how optimized your actual site is. Mid-tier, reputable hosting is usually worth the modest extra cost for the speed and reliability difference.

5. Minimize redirects and unnecessary scripts

Each redirect and each third-party script (trackers, widgets) adds a small delay. Auditing what's actually necessary versus what's accumulated over time can meaningfully improve load speed.

How to check your current speed

Free tools exist that test any URL and highlight specific issues — running your own site through one is a good first step before making changes, so you can measure the actual improvement afterward.

What's a good page load time to aim for?

Under 2-3 seconds for the page to become usable is a reasonable target for most business websites.

Do these fixes require a developer?

Image compression and app/plugin audits can often be done without technical help; hosting changes and deeper technical fixes sometimes benefit from professional support.
