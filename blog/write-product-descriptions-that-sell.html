How to Write Product Descriptions That Actually Sell

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 5 min read
Product descriptions sell better when they lead with the customer's problem or desire rather than just specs, use specific sensory or concrete details instead of generic adjectives, and end with a clear reason to buy now rather than just a feature list.

Specs tell, stories sell

"100% cotton, machine washable" tells a customer what the product is made of. It doesn't tell them why they should care. Good product descriptions translate features into what they actually mean for the person reading — not machine washable, but "still looks new after fifty washes."

Lead with the problem or desire, not the product

Instead of opening with what the product is, open with what the customer is trying to solve or feel. A skincare product description that opens with "wake up to skin that actually looks rested" connects faster than one that opens with a list of ingredients.

Use specific, concrete language over generic adjectives

"High quality" and "amazing" are vague enough to mean nothing to a skeptical reader. "Hand-stitched with double-reinforced seams" is specific enough to actually build trust, because it's a detail that would be strange to make up.

Address the objection before they think of it

If a product has a common hesitation attached to it (fit, sizing, shipping time, return policy), address it briefly right in the description rather than making the customer go hunting for that reassurance elsewhere.

End with a reason to act now

A description that simply describes the product and stops leaves the decision entirely to the reader's own motivation. A short closing line — limited stock, a seasonal use case, a satisfaction guarantee — gives a gentle final push toward the actual purchase decision.

A simple structure that works for most products

  1. One sentence: the core benefit or feeling
  2. Two to three sentences: specific details that build trust and answer likely questions
  3. One sentence: the gentle push to buy now
How long should a product description actually be?

Long enough to answer real questions and build trust, short enough that it doesn't feel like a chore to read — often 3-6 sentences for most products, longer for higher-consideration purchases.

Should product descriptions be optimized for SEO too?

Yes — naturally including the terms customers actually search for helps the product page get found, as long as it doesn't come at the cost of readability.
