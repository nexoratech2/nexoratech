5 SEO Myths That Are Hurting Your Website Rankings

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 5 min read
The biggest SEO myths still circulating are that keyword stuffing helps rankings, that more pages always means better SEO, that meta keywords still matter, that backlinks from anywhere help, and that SEO is a one-time task rather than ongoing work.

Myth 1: Keyword stuffing improves rankings

Repeating a keyword unnaturally throughout a page used to work over a decade ago — today it actively hurts rankings and reads poorly to actual visitors. Modern search engines reward natural language that clearly answers the searcher's question, not repetition.

Myth 2: More pages always means better SEO

Thin, low-value pages created just to "have more content" can actually drag down a site's overall quality signal. A smaller number of genuinely useful, well-written pages consistently outperforms a large volume of shallow ones.

Myth 3: The meta keywords tag still matters

Google stopped using the meta keywords tag as a ranking factor a long time ago. Time spent stuffing it with keyword lists is time better spent on the actual page content and meta description, which still matters for click-through rate.

Myth 4: Any backlink helps, regardless of source

Links from low-quality, spammy, or entirely unrelated sites can actually harm rankings rather than help. A smaller number of links from relevant, reputable sources is far more valuable than a large volume from anywhere that will link back.

Myth 5: SEO is a one-time task

Search algorithms change, competitors publish new content, and your own business evolves — SEO that worked well a year ago can quietly lose ground if it's never revisited. Treating it as ongoing maintenance, not a one-time project, is what actually sustains rankings.

What actually moves the needle

Clear, genuinely useful content that directly answers real questions your customers search for, a technically clean and fast site, and a small number of quality backlinks — consistently, over time.

Do these myths still get recommended by some SEO providers?

Unfortunately yes — some outdated advice persists because it was true years ago. It's worth questioning any SEO plan built entirely around volume (pages, links, keywords) rather than genuine quality.

How long does it take to recover from an SEO mistake like keyword stuffing?

Once fixed, recovery timelines vary but often take a few months to fully reflect in rankings — another reason to avoid these mistakes in the first place.
