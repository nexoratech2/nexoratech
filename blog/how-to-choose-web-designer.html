How to Choose the Right Web Designer for Your Business

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 6 min read
Choose a web designer by reviewing their live portfolio (not mockups), asking directly about post-launch support and revision policy, confirming they build with SEO fundamentals included, and checking that their communication style matches how you actually want to work.

Start with their portfolio — but look closely

Ask for links to live, currently functioning websites they've built, not just static images or mockups. Visit them on your phone, click around, check if they still load quickly and look current. A portfolio full of screenshots but no live links is a red flag worth asking about directly.

Ask about what happens after launch

A surprising number of website projects end abruptly at launch, leaving the business owner unsure how to make even small updates. Ask specifically: is there a support period included? What happens if something breaks a month later? Is ongoing maintenance available if I want it?

Confirm SEO is actually included, not an afterthought

A site that looks great but was built without basic SEO structure (proper headings, meta tags, mobile optimization, reasonable load speed) will underperform regardless of design quality. Ask directly what SEO fundamentals are included in the build, not added later as a separate service.

Check communication style and responsiveness

Before signing anything, notice how they communicate during the sales conversation itself — are they clear, responsive, and willing to explain things in plain language? That's usually a strong predictor of what working together will actually feel like.

Understand the revision and scope process

Ask how revisions work — is there a set number included, or is it open-ended? Vague answers here often lead to scope creep or unexpected additional charges later in the project.

Red flags worth taking seriously

  • No live examples of past work, only images
  • Vague or evasive answers about pricing and what's included
  • Guaranteed specific SEO rankings (no one can honestly guarantee this)
  • Pressure to decide immediately without time to review a proposal
Should I always choose the cheapest quote?

Not automatically — compare what's actually included in each quote, not just the headline number. A cheaper quote missing SEO setup or support may cost more later.

How many portfolio examples should I review before deciding?

Enough to see a consistent pattern of quality — usually 3-5 live sites gives a fair picture of someone's real skill level and style.
