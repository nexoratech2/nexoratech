Google Ads vs Meta Ads: Which Should You Start With?

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 5 min read
Start with Google Ads if customers are actively searching for what you sell (high purchase intent); start with Meta Ads if you need to build awareness and reach people who aren't yet searching but match your ideal customer profile.

The core difference in how each platform works

Google Ads shows up when someone actively searches for something — meaning you're reaching people who already know they want a solution and are looking for it right now. Meta Ads (Facebook/Instagram) shows up while people are scrolling, not actively searching — meaning you're introducing your business to people who may not know they need it yet.

When Google Ads is the better starting point

If your product or service is something people actively search for by name or need ("emergency plumber near me," "buy running shoes online"), Google Ads captures that existing intent directly. This tends to convert faster since the customer is already in a buying mindset.

When Meta Ads is the better starting point

If your product is newer, more visual, or something people don't yet know they want (a new type of product, a lifestyle brand, an impulse-buy item), Meta's targeting by interests and behaviors is better suited to introducing it to the right audience before they'd think to search for it.

Cost and competition considerations

Google Ads costs can be higher per click in competitive, high-intent categories (legal, insurance, home services), since you're bidding directly against competitors for the same searches. Meta Ads often has a lower cost per impression but requires stronger creative (images/video) to actually stop the scroll and earn attention.

A reasonable way to decide

Ask: are people already actively searching for what I sell? If yes, start with Google Ads. Is my product something people need to see and understand visually before wanting it? If yes, start with Meta Ads. Many mature businesses eventually run both, but it's rarely necessary to start with both at once.

Which platform is cheaper for a small budget?

It varies heavily by industry — highly competitive search terms can be expensive on Google, while Meta often allows testing at a lower minimum spend, but results depend more on creative quality.

Can I run both at the same time on a small budget?

It's usually better to start with one platform, learn what works, and expand to the second once you have some data and a working budget structure — spreading a small budget too thin across both makes it hard to learn from either.
