Do You Need Both SEO and Paid Ads, or Just One?

By Afolabi Oluwaseyi Christopher · July 2026 · 5 min read
If you need customers immediately, start with paid ads; if you're building for the long term and can invest patience upfront, prioritize SEO — most established businesses eventually benefit from running both together, but rarely need to start with both at once.

The fundamental tradeoff between the two

Paid ads deliver traffic the moment you turn them on — and traffic stops the moment you turn them off. SEO takes months to build momentum, but that momentum tends to compound and persist even without ongoing daily spend, once a page is well-ranked.

When paid ads should come first

  • You need revenue now — a new product launch, a seasonal business, cash flow pressure
  • You want to test messaging and offers quickly before investing in long-term content
  • You're in an extremely competitive SEO niche where ranking will take a long time regardless

When SEO should come first

  • You're building a business meant to last years, not just months
  • Your budget is limited and needs to stretch — SEO's cost-per-customer drops over time in a way ads never do
  • You have the patience to invest 3-6 months before seeing meaningful returns

Why most mature businesses eventually run both

Paid ads provide the immediate, controllable traffic. SEO provides the compounding, lower-cost-over-time traffic. Together, they cover both the short-term revenue need and the long-term cost efficiency — which is why most growing businesses aren't choosing one over the other permanently, just sequencing which comes first based on their immediate situation.

A simple way to decide where to start

If you could survive the next 6 months without new customers while you build organic visibility, start with SEO. If you genuinely can't, start with paid ads to bridge the gap, and layer SEO in alongside it once cash flow allows.

Can I do both from day one on a small budget?

It's possible, but spreading a small budget too thin across both often means neither gets the sustained investment needed to actually work — sequencing usually produces better early results.

Does having paid ads help my SEO, or vice versa?

They don't directly boost each other's rankings, but paid ads can validate which messaging converts, which then informs stronger, more effective SEO content.
